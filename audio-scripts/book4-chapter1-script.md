## CHAPTER ONE: THE INTERVIEW

### PART 1: LILY

*[Sound: Elevator ascending, soft mechanical hum]*

---

**NARRATOR (LILY):**

The elevator to the ninety-second floor of Cole Tower took exactly forty-seven seconds. I knew because I counted every single one, trying to slow my racing heart with mathematics instead of the breathing exercises my therapist had taught me.

*[Beat]*

*Forty-five. Forty-six. Forty-seven.*

*[Sound: Elevator ding]*

The doors slid open, and I stepped into a world I'd only ever seen in magazines.

Floor-to-ceiling windows framed Manhattan like a living painting—the Hudson River glittering in the afternoon sun, the city sprawling beneath us like it existed solely for the viewing pleasure of whoever lived here. The furniture was all clean lines and neutral tones, expensive in that understated way that screamed old money. Or new money trying very hard to look old.

*[Footsteps on hardwood]*

"Miss Chen?"

I turned to find a woman in her fifties approaching, her silver hair pulled back in an elegant chignon, her smile warm but assessing. The kind of smile that said she'd seen a hundred nannies come through these doors and was already calculating how long I'd last.

"I'm Margaret. Mr. Cole's household manager." She extended her hand. "Thank you for coming on such short notice."

*[Sound: Brief handshake]*

"Of course." I kept my voice steady, professional. "The agency said it was urgent."

Margaret's smile flickered. "That's... one word for it. The last nanny quit yesterday. Without notice. Emma's had four caregivers in the past eight months."

*[Pause]*

Four nannies in eight months. For a two-year-old. That wasn't a red flag—it was a crimson banner waving frantically in the wind.

"May I ask why?"

Margaret hesitated, glancing toward a hallway that presumably led to the private quarters. "Mr. Cole is... particular. About Emma's care. About everything, really. He has high standards."

*[Internal thought, softer]*

*High standards.* The polite way of saying *impossible to please.*

I should have turned around right then. Should have smiled politely, made my excuses, and taken that forty-seven-second elevator ride back to reality. Back to my tiny apartment in Queens and my mountain of student debt and the temp jobs that barely covered rent.

But then I heard it.

*[Sound: A child crying, muffled but distinct]*

A small voice, somewhere down that hallway. Not screaming—worse. The quiet, hiccupping sobs of a child who'd learned that crying loudly didn't bring anyone running.

*[Beat]*

"Is that Emma?"

Margaret's composure cracked, just slightly. "She's been... difficult today. Since the last nanny left, she won't let anyone—"

I was already moving.

### PART 2: ETHAN

*[Sound: Office ambiance—keyboard clicking, muffled phone conversation]*

---

**NARRATOR (ETHAN):**

The quarterly projections were a disaster.

I stared at the spreadsheet on my screen, watching the numbers blur together as my mind wandered—again—to the crying I'd heard through my office door twenty minutes ago. Emma. My daughter. The tiny human I'd helped create but had no idea how to comfort.

*[Sound: Intercom buzz]*

"Mr. Cole?" Margaret's voice crackled through. "The new nanny candidate is here. Miss Chen."

*[Sigh]*

"Send her in."

I minimized the spreadsheet and pulled up her file instead. Lily Chen. Twenty-six. Master's degree in early childhood development from Columbia. Glowing references from her previous position at a Montessori school in Brooklyn. On paper, she was perfect.

They were all perfect on paper.

*[Sound: Door opening]*

I looked up, prepared to deliver my standard speech about expectations and boundaries and the non-negotiable nature of Emma's routine—

And forgot every word.

*[Pause]*

She wasn't what I expected. The agency usually sent women in sensible cardigans and practical shoes, women who looked like they'd stepped out of a Mary Poppins casting call. This woman wore a simple navy dress that somehow made her look both professional and... soft. Her dark hair was pulled back, but a few strands had escaped, framing a face that was striking in its openness. No artifice. No calculation.

And she was holding my daughter.

*[Beat]*

Emma—my Emma, who screamed when strangers came near her, who hadn't let anyone but Margaret hold her in weeks—was curled against this woman's shoulder, her small fist clutching the collar of that navy dress, her sobs reduced to occasional shuddering breaths.

*[Sound: Soft child's breathing]*

"Mr. Cole." The woman's voice was calm, steady. "I'm Lily Chen. I apologize for the unconventional introduction, but Emma needed—"

"How did you do that?"

The words came out sharper than I intended. Accusatory. Because in two years of watching my daughter flinch away from everyone who tried to care for her, I'd never seen her go willingly into anyone's arms.

*[Pause]*

Lily Chen met my eyes without flinching. "I sat on the floor outside her room and waited. When she was ready, she came to me."

*[Beat]*

"You... waited."

"Children aren't puzzles to be solved, Mr. Cole. They're people who need to feel safe." She shifted Emma slightly, and my daughter—my guarded, traumatized, untrusting daughter—*nuzzled* into her neck. "She doesn't feel safe. That's not a behavioral problem. That's a response to her environment."

*[Long pause]*

I should have been offended. Should have reminded her that I was the employer here, that I hadn't asked for her assessment of my parenting or my home. But I couldn't stop staring at Emma's face—peaceful for the first time in days—and the woman who'd somehow accomplished in minutes what I'd failed to do in months.

*[Sound: Chair scraping back]*

"You're hired."

Lily blinked. "Don't you want to discuss terms? References? My approach to—"

"You made my daughter stop crying." I stood, moving around the desk toward them. "Whatever your salary requirements are, I'll double them. Whatever schedule you need, it's yours. Just..." 

*[Voice softening, vulnerable]*

"Just don't leave her."

*[Beat]*

Something shifted in Lily Chen's expression. Something that looked almost like compassion. Or maybe pity. I couldn't tell the difference anymore.

"I don't leave, Mr. Cole." She held my gaze. "But I have conditions."

*[Pause]*

"Name them."

"Emma needs consistency. Routine. And she needs *you*—not just your money or your staff. She needs her father present. Can you do that?"

*[Long silence]*

No one had ever spoken to me like that. Not my board, not my lawyers, not the parade of nannies who'd smiled and nodded and then fled at the first sign of difficulty.

*[Sound: Emma stirring, small sigh]*

I looked at my daughter. At her tiny fingers still clutching Lily's dress. At the peace on her face that I hadn't been able to give her.

"Yes." The word felt like a promise. Like a prayer. "I can do that."
