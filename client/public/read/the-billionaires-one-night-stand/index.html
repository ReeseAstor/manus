About the book

One night. No souvenirs. No strings on Monday.

Victoria Cruz gave her firm seven years of eighty-hour weeks and its biggest win of the year. They made the man with the right father partner instead. So she takes one night for herself, on her own terms, with a stranger who doesn't do feelings, doesn't do touch and doesn't lose.

James Chen agrees to every rule she sets. He just doesn't mention that his company has spent a month getting ready to buy her firm.

On Monday, he owns it. Her career is on his table. His secrets are in her notes. He could end everything she built. She could expose everything he's hiding. And neither of them can stop wanting the one person who could ruin them.

A steamy workplace novella with a billionaire hero who learns to listen, a heroine who refuses to be acquired, and a hard-won HEA. A Manhattan Money Kings standalone. Explicit, open-door scenes. For readers 18 and over.

Chapter One: Victoria

Read the opening

The conference room smelled like Clive Christian cologne and betrayal, and I’d known which one was winning before I sat down.

Bradley Whitmore was taking his victory lap. Down the length of the mahogany, palm to palm with every senior partner, that practiced grip, that jaw built for a campaign poster. Three hundred fewer billable hours than mine last quarter. A father who golfed with Judge Albrecht. A Princeton pedigree and the easy confidence of a man who had never once done the math on whether he belonged in a room.

I’d done the math my whole life. Seven years of eighty-hour weeks. A pro bono docket they loved to quote in the annual report, progressive optics billed to my nights and weekends.

“Victoria.” Walter Lowell found me across the lacquered slab, sympathy stretched thin over arithmetic. The look you give a racehorse before the auction. “Your work on Halstead was exceptional. The committee wanted me to personally acknowledge—”

“Thank you, Walter.” Calm. Precise. Already decided, the way a closing argument is decided before you stand. “Bradley will be an asset.”

Asset. Not partner. Not equal. A line item.

My jaw stayed loose. My fingers stayed flat against the leather portfolio instead of curling into it. Six partners watched from their Aerons, waiting for the small, satisfying fracture that would let them call this merit.

Her stories run on banter, high stakes, family empires and the kind of heat that fogs the windows, and every one ends in a hard-won happily ever after.
