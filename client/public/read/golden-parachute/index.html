The story

About the book

He can restructure a billion dollars of bad debt. He cannot buy his way around the one woman who refuses to be managed.

Miri Doyle-Levine has spent eight years keeping other people on their feet. A home health aide fighting her way into nursing school, she has no time for a man who treats every problem like a number he can make disappear.

Kai Hsu built his life the way he handles distressed credit: control the risk, close the exits, never need anything. Then Miri sees through him in one glance.

She refuses his money. He keeps trying to turn care into logistics. And when the body he has ignored for years forces him to stop, both of them have to decide what love looks like when rescue is off the table.

Hudson Dynasty Book Three. A complete standalone with its own HEA. Explicit, open-door scenes. Intended for readers 18 and over.

One: Miri

Read the opening

Arthur Klein’s insulin pen quit working under the most expensive chandelier in Manhattan, and I had about ninety seconds to fix it before the room noticed a man was in trouble.

One hand in my tote. Two fingers on the spare pen cap. My shoulder wedged between Arthur’s wheelchair and a marble side table holding twelve thousand dollars of orchids nobody was supposed to breathe on. Behind me, a woman in winter-white silk laughed at a joke from a man whose cuff links cost more than the envelope in my bag. BMCC Nursing Program. Spring deposit due in nineteen days. I had been carrying it for nine days the way other people carry a diagnosis.

“If I die at a donor reception, Miriam,” Arthur said, “tell my daughter I blame the salmon mousse.”

“You are not dying. You are dramatic.” I kept my voice low and my smile in place, because rich rooms get nervous when staff look worried. “Also, you were warned about the mousse. Twice.”

“It looked lonely.”

“Food with capers is never lonely. Hold still.”

Reese Astor writes steamy contemporary romance set in the glass towers of Manhattan, where powerful men meet women sharp enough to bring them to their knees. Her stories run on banter, high stakes, family empires and the kind of heat that fogs the windows, and every one ends in a hard-won happily ever after.
