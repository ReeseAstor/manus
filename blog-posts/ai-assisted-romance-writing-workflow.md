# How I Write a 90,000-Word Romance Novel in 30 Days (My AI Workflow, Unfiltered)

I get this question constantly: "Reese, how do you publish so fast without burning out?"

The honest answer? I built a system. Not a shortcut — a system. One that lets me stay in the creative driver's seat while offloading the parts of novel-writing that used to drain me dry.

I'm going to walk you through my actual production workflow. The one I use for the Hudson Dynasty series. No gatekeeping, no fluff.

## First, Let's Kill the Myth

Using AI in your writing process doesn't mean you press a button and a book appears. That's not how this works. That's not how any of this works.

What AI does — when you set it up correctly — is act like having five specialized editors sitting in your office, available 24/7, who never get tired and never judge you for rewriting the same scene six times at 2 AM.

My process still requires me to:
- Develop every character from scratch
- Plot every twist and turn
- Write the emotional beats that make readers cry
- Make creative decisions AI literally cannot make

What changes is the *speed* at which I can iterate, audit, and polish.

## The 10-Workflow System

I broke my writing process into ten distinct workflows, organized across seven production phases. Each one handles a specific job.

### Phase 0: Control (Before You Write a Single Word)

This is where most authors skip ahead and regret it later. I maintain two control documents:

**The Master Production Tracker** keeps every thread in the series organized — who knows what, which evidence has been planted, where each romance arc sits on the tension curve. After every chapter, I update it. Non-negotiable.

**The Canon Control system** prevents me from accidentally contradicting something I established three books ago. When you're writing a five-book series with interconnected characters, corporate conspiracies, and multiple romance arcs happening simultaneously, continuity errors are the enemy.

### Phase 1: Architecture

Before I draft a single chapter, I build the blueprint. This isn't a loose outline — it's a full structural document with:

- The premise engine (what drives every scene forward)
- Three-act structure with specific turning points
- A 24-chapter beat table
- A heat matrix that maps exactly where intimacy escalates

Think of it like building a skyscraper. You don't start pouring concrete without blueprints. Same principle.

### Phase 2: Chapter Build

Each chapter gets its own "card" before I write it. POV character, scene goal, central conflict, the turn that changes everything, which artifacts or clues get planted, where the romance pressure sits, and the ending hook that makes readers flip to the next chapter.

This takes me about 15 minutes per chapter. But it saves hours of rewriting later.

### Phase 3: Draft

This is where the actual writing happens. I draft in scene units — each scene has a specific job, and I know what that job is before I start typing.

After the raw draft, I run an anti-AI pass. This is crucial. I strip out every phrase that sounds generated, every construction that feels algorithmic. My readers are smart. They can tell. So I replace clinical precision with messy humanity — the kind of voice that makes a reader feel like they're inside Nia's head as she's falling for a man she should hate.

### Phase 4: Section Audits

Every 4-6 chapters, I pause and audit two things:

**Continuity & Suspense:** Does the mystery still track? Is the evidence chain logical? Would a careful reader spot a plot hole?

**Romance & Heat:** Is the chemistry building organically or does it feel bolted on? Are the intimate scenes earning their place in the story or just filling a quota?

### Phase 5: Full Manuscript QC

Once the draft is complete, I score the entire manuscript across 16 categories. Pacing, dialogue authenticity, emotional resonance, plot logic, heat calibration — all of it gets a number. Anything below a 7 gets flagged for revision.

This is the step that turns a good book into one that readers message me about at midnight saying "I just finished and I'm WRECKED."

### Phase 6: Package

The book is written. Now it needs to sell. I generate multiple blurb versions, test different hooks, nail down the keywords and categories, and craft ad copy that stops a reader mid-scroll.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

For *Hostile Tender* (Hudson Dynasty Book 1), this system took me from concept to KDP-ready manuscript in 28 days. That includes:

- 4 days of architecture and planning
- 16 days of drafting (averaging 5,600 words/day)
- 5 days of audits and revisions
- 3 days of packaging and launch prep

Compare that to my pre-system timeline of 4-6 months per book. Same quality — my ARC readers confirmed that — but dramatically faster execution.

## The Part Nobody Talks About

Here's what I've learned after running this system across multiple books: the AI doesn't make you a better writer. *You* make you a better writer. The AI just removes the friction between your creative vision and the finished product.

It's like the difference between hand-calculating your taxes and using software. The tax knowledge is still yours. The decisions are still yours. But you're not spending three weekends buried in arithmetic.

## Should You Build a System Like This?

If you're writing standalone contemporaries with simple plots, you probably don't need ten workflows. Maybe three or four would serve you.

But if you're writing:
- Connected series with recurring characters
- Complex plots with mystery or suspense elements
- Multiple POVs with interweaving timelines
- Books that need to maintain consistency across 400,000+ words of series content

Then yes. Build the system. Your future self will thank you.
