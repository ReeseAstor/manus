# My Actual Reading Self-Care Routine (Not the Instagram Version)

*Everyone posts the aesthetic flat-lay with the latte art and the cashmere throw. Here's what my reading routine actually looks like — and the few things that genuinely make it better.*

I want to be clear about something: most "reading routines" you see online are styled for the photo. The reality of being a heavy reader is less photogenic. It's reading on the toilet. It's reading while stirring pasta. It's reading in the car at pickup because you have three chapters left and you physically cannot stop. The aesthetic reading night is real, but it's maybe 20% of my actual reading life.

That said — the 20% matters. Having a deliberate wind-down routine that involves reading has genuinely improved my sleep, my stress levels, and my relationship with books. So here's what I actually do, stripped of the performance.

## The Weeknight Version (30 Minutes)

This is what happens on a regular Tuesday when I have nothing special planned but I want to read before bed without just scrolling my phone until I pass out.

**8:30 PM**: I put my phone in another room. This is the single most impactful thing I've done for my reading life. Not on the nightstand. Not face-down on the bed. In another room. The first week was genuinely difficult. Now it's automatic.

**8:35 PM**: I light whatever candle is on my nightstand. Right now it's a [library-scented one](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B087YPT934?tag=88away0d-20) — old paper, leather, a hint of vanilla. I've burned through probably fifteen of these over the past two years. The scent has become a trigger: I smell it and my brain shifts into reading mode. Pavlovian, but it works.

**8:40 PM**: I get into bed with my [Kindle Paperwhite](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CFPJYX2T?tag=88away0d-20), warm light on, regular light off. The room is dim. The candle is flickering. I'm comfortable. This is when the day actually ends for me — not when I finished work, not when I finished dinner, but when I open my book.

**9:00-9:30 PM**: I read until I'm sleepy. Sometimes that's twenty minutes. Sometimes it's two hours if the book is particularly good. I don't set a timer. I don't force myself to stop at a specific time. I just read until my eyes get heavy, then I blow out the candle and sleep.

That's it. No elaborate skincare routine happening simultaneously. No journaling. No meditation app playing in the background. Just a candle, a book, and the absence of my phone.

## The Weekend Version (The Full Experience)

Friday or Saturday night, when I have nowhere to be the next morning, I go harder.

**The Bath**: I run a hot bath with whatever bath salts are under the sink. Nothing fancy — I buy the big bags from the drugstore. The Kindle comes with me (it's waterproof, and I've tested this extensively). I prop it on the edge of the tub or hold it above the water. I've been doing this for years without incident, but I understand the anxiety. A [waterproof phone pouch](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B085HN5DPG?tag=88away0d-20) works too if you read on your phone and want protection.

**The Setup**: After the bath, I build what I privately call "the nest." This involves: my bed, every pillow I own arranged for maximum support, a [weighted blanket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C1LBXLG4?tag=88away0d-20) (I resisted these for years and now I'm a convert — the gentle pressure is genuinely calming), and a cup of something warm. Usually herbal tea. Sometimes hot chocolate if the book calls for it.

**The Reading**: I read for as long as I want. No guilt about staying up late. No alarm set for the morning. This is when I finish books — when I read the last hundred pages in one sitting because nothing is pulling me away. These are the nights I ugly-cry over fictional characters and feel zero embarrassment about it.

## The Things That Actually Improved My Reading Life

I've tried a lot of "reading accessories" over the years. Most are unnecessary. These few genuinely changed something:

### The Kindle Paperwhite

I've mentioned it already, but the [Kindle Paperwhite](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CFPJYX2T?tag=88away0d-20) is the foundation of everything. Before I had one, I read on my phone, which meant every reading session was interrupted by notifications. The Kindle does one thing — displays books — and that single-purpose nature is its greatest feature. No notifications. No temptation to check email. Just the story.

### A Dedicated Reading Light

For physical book nights (because sometimes you want paper), the [Glocusent book light](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07WNRN9WQ?tag=88away0d-20) clips onto the cover and provides warm, adjustable light without disturbing anyone else in the room. I use it maybe twice a week when I'm reading a physical ARC or a book I bought specifically for the beautiful cover.

### The Right Candle

I'm particular about this. The candle can't be too strong (headache), can't be floral (distracting), and can't be food-scented (makes me hungry). Library, leather, sandalwood, cedar — these are reading scents. They're warm and grounding without demanding attention. I keep two or three on rotation: a [classical library candle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B087YPT934?tag=88away0d-20) for most nights, and something with cedar and tobacco for darker reads.

### Comfortable Reading Position

This sounds obvious but most people read in positions that cause neck strain, back pain, or arm fatigue. I spent actual time figuring out my optimal reading position: slightly reclined (not flat), pillow behind my lower back, Kindle held at eye level (not in my lap — that's how you get neck problems). A [reading pillow](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BVNLF7GY?tag=88away0d-20) with arms helps if you read sitting up in bed. It looks ridiculous. It works perfectly.

## What I Don't Do

I don't track my reading publicly. I don't post my "currently reading" on Instagram. I don't feel obligated to finish books I'm not enjoying. I don't read a minimum number of pages per day. I don't participate in reading challenges that turn pleasure into obligation.

Reading is the one area of my life that I refuse to optimize, gamify, or perform. It's mine. The routine exists to protect that — to create a container where reading is just reading, not content, not productivity, not self-improvement.

## The Unexpected Benefit

Since establishing this routine — phone in another room, candle lit, Kindle open — my sleep has improved dramatically. Not because reading is boring (it's not; I regularly stay up too late), but because the routine signals to my brain that the day is over. The blue light is gone. The stimulation of social media is gone. What's left is a story, a warm light, and permission to rest.

I didn't start this routine for sleep. I started it because I wanted to read more. The sleep improvement was a side effect. But it's the reason I've maintained it for years, even on nights when I'm not particularly in the mood to read. The routine itself has value independent of the books.

## Building Your Own Version

You don't need to copy mine. The specifics don't matter. What matters is:

1. **Remove the phone.** Physically. From the room.
2. **Create a sensory trigger.** A candle, a specific tea, a playlist — something that tells your brain "we're reading now."
3. **Make it comfortable.** If your body is uncomfortable, your mind won't settle into the story.
4. **Protect it.** Don't let other obligations creep in. This time is yours.

Start with ten minutes. You'll naturally extend it once the habit takes hold. And if you're looking for the book to start with — something that will make you forget the time and read longer than you planned — the Manhattan Money Kings series has been known to keep readers up well past their intended bedtime.
