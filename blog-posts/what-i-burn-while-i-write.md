# What I Burn While I Write: My Author Candle Collection

*I'm a romance author and a candle person — here's every candle on my writing desk, what each one smells like, and when I burn it.*

I have a confession: I judge books by their smell.

Not literally, but close. When I write a scene set in a Manhattan penthouse, I want my study to smell like a Manhattan penthouse. When I'm drafting a scene into the AM, I want the air to smell like focus. Scent is the fastest way I know to *set the mood in a room*, and for someone who spends ten-hour days inventing worlds, mood is everything.

So here's the honest inventory of my writing desk candle collection — what I burn, when, and why. Plus a few I'm testing for the next newsletter.

## The Everyday Writing Candle: My Signature

The one candle that never leaves my desk is the [Classical Library Candle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F1FY6XT7?tag=88away0d-20). It's a soy wax candle with notes of aged leather, mahogany, and old paper — designed, I'm convinced, to smell like the library I want to live in.

I light this one the second I sit down to write. There's something about the scent that tells my brain *it's time*. By the time the room smells like a grand library, my protagonist is usually telling me what she wants.

Close your eyes, light this candle, and suddenly you're in the library of a Manhattan penthouse. Pure magic. It's also the candle I recommend when readers ask what to burn during a reading night — it works for both sides of the book.

## The Deadline Candle: A Little Humor at 1 AM

Every author has a night where the chapter is *almost* done and the words have left the building. On those nights I light the [“Smells Like She's Writing a Bestseller” Soy Candle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CMTXKBWW?tag=88away0d-20).

The label, when I'm lying in bed at 1 AM, might still be aspirational. But the scent — warm, cinnamony, sweet — is the kind of comfort that keeps you going when the plot has wandered off. It's also my favorite gift to give other authors, because every writer needs to smell like the thing they're chasing.

## The Seasonal Rotation

Just like anyone else, I flip my candles with the calendar:

- **Fall drafting season**: mahogany and leather notes, heavier wood — the darker stuff. Autumn in the moneymaking world is suspense season.
- **Winter**: lighter, sweeter — cotton and vanilla. My read-and-rewrite season, when warmth matters more than edge.
- **Spring/summer**: clean, bright — bergamot or cedar. Fast-paced contest season, when my brain needs to move.

## When I Don't Burn a Candle

One honest note: I don't burn anything while I'm doing line edits. Editing is precision work, and for that I want zero distraction — just the hum of the office and the screen. Candles are for drafting and reading; silence is for surgery.

## My Candles vs. My Favorite Book Smells

There's a joke among romance authors that we're all secretly smell-obsessed. It's why so many of my favorite bookish moments involve a character walking into a space and *inhaling* — a leather-and-cedar billionaire's loft, a bakery in a small town, rain on Manhattan asphalt.

The Classical Library candle is as close as I've found to bottling the *idea* of those rooms. If the Billionaire's Executive Assistant ever had an apartment smell, it would be this: old money, new heat, and the patience of someone who settles for nothing.

## Final Thoughts

Candles are the cheapest mood-setter an author owns. Two good ones — one for drafting, one for deadlines — have quietly changed how I write.

If you want to smell exactly what my writing desk smells like, start with the [Classical Library Candle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F1FY6XT7?tag=88away0d-20) and the ["Smells Like She's Writing a Bestseller" Soy Candle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CMTXKBWW?tag=88away0d-20). And if you burn them while reading my books? We're officially on the same wavelength.

Light one, close your eyes, and disappear into the story.
